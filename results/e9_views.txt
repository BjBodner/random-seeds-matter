rule of thumb at about 80% accuracy: swing = 40 / sqrt(n) points: n = 100 -> 4.0, n = 400 -> 2.0, n = 1600 -> 1.0, n = 10000 -> 0.4

== 4. The tie rule: only the rows where two models disagree can pick a winner ==
Among d disagreements, the lead (rows won by A minus rows won by B) is a fair-coin count with a typical size of sqrt(d) rows if the models are really equal. A lead of less than 2 * sqrt(d) rows is inside the usual 5% bar: call it a tie.
(A lead has the same odd/even parity as d. This is the 5% bar for one planned comparison, not for the best of several tries.)
d = 15 disagreements on 300 rows: 2 * sqrt(15) = 7.7 rows, so a lead of 7 rows or fewer (2.3 points) is a tie
d = 100 disagreements on 1000 rows: 2 * sqrt(100) = 20.0 rows, so a lead of 18 rows or fewer (1.8 points) is a tie
example: 9 wins to 6 on d = 15 (a lead of 3): a fair coin gives 9 or more heads in 15 flips 30.4% of the time
